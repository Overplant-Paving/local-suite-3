❤️ CPR — no breathing / no pulse

Call 911 first (or have someone call) and get an AED if available. Start CPR if the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally.
Push to the beat — 100–120 compressions/min. (This tone is ~110/min.)

Adult

  1. Kneel beside them. Place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest, other hand on top, fingers interlocked.
  2. Push hard and fast, at least 2 inches deep, letting the chest fully recoil between pushes.
  3. Keep the rhythm at 100–120/min (the metronome above). Minimize pauses.
  4. If trained and willing: 30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths. Otherwise hands-only CPR is fine — just keep pushing.
  5. Continue until the person moves/breathes, an AED/medic takes over, or you're too exhausted to continue.

Child (1 year to puberty)

  1. Use one or two hands in the center of the chest. Push about 2 inches deep.
  2. Same 100–120/min rhythm. If trained: 30:2 (or 15:2 with two rescuers).
  3. If alone and unwitnessed collapse, give 2 minutes of CPR before leaving to call 911.

Infant (under 1 year)

  1. Use two fingers (or two thumbs encircling the chest) just below the nipple line.
  2. Push about 1.5 inches deep, 100–120/min.
  3. If trained: 30:2 (single rescuer). Be gentle but firm; full recoil between pushes.
Untrained and unsure? A 911 dispatcher can talk you through CPR step by step — stay on the phone.

🫁 Choking

If they can cough forcefully or speak, encourage coughing — don't interfere. Act when they can't breathe, cough, or make sound.

Adult or child (responsive)

  1. Give 5 back blows: bend them forward, strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  2. Give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich): fist just above the navel, grasp with other hand, thrust inward and upward.
  3. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 thrusts until the object clears or they become unresponsive.
  4. If they go unresponsive: lower them safely, call 911, and start CPR (check the mouth for a visible object before breaths).

Infant (under 1)

  1. Face-down along your forearm, head lower than body: 5 back blows between the shoulder blades.
  2. Turn face-up: 5 chest thrusts with two fingers (as in infant CPR).
  3. Repeat until cleared or unresponsive — then start infant CPR and call 911.
Pregnant or very large person: give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.

🩸 Severe bleeding

  1. Call 911 for heavy or spurting bleeding. Protect yourself (gloves if available).
  2. Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or gauze right on the wound.
  3. Do not lift the cloth to peek — if it soaks through, add more on top and keep pressing.
  4. If it doesn't stop and you have a tourniquet, apply it 2–3 inches above the wound (not on a joint), tighten until bleeding stops, and note the time.
  5. Keep the person warm and lying down; treat for shock. Stay with them until help arrives.
Embedded object? Don't pull it out — press around it and pad to hold it in place.

🔥 Burns

  1. Cool it: run cool (not ice-cold) water over the burn for 10–20 minutes. Remove rings/tight clothing near the area before it swells.
  2. Do not apply ice, butter, oil, or toothpaste. Don't break blisters.
  3. Cover loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing or cling film.
  4. Over-the-counter pain relief helps. Keep the person warm overall.
Call 911 / seek ER care for burns that are: larger than the person's palm, on the face/hands/feet/genitals or over a joint, deep/white/charred, from chemicals or electricity, or on a child or older adult.

🧠 Stroke — think FAST

  • F — Face: Ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
  • A — Arms: Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
  • S — Speech: Ask them to repeat a simple sentence. Is it slurred or strange?
  • T — Time: Any one of these? Call 911 immediately and note the time symptoms started.
Note the time last known well — treatment options depend on it. Don't give food, drink, or medication.

💔 Heart attack

Common signs: chest pressure/pain/squeezing; discomfort in arm(s), jaw, neck, or back; shortness of breath; cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness. Signs can be subtler in women, older adults, and people with diabetes.

  1. Call 911 now — don't drive yourself if you can avoid it.
  2. Have the person rest and stay calm; loosen tight clothing.
  3. If they're not allergic and it isn't advised against, an aspirin (chew 1 adult or 4 low-dose) can help — a dispatcher will advise.
  4. If they become unresponsive and aren't breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED if available.
