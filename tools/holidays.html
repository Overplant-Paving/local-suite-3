Federal holidays

The 11 U.S. federal holidays. When a fixed-date holiday lands on a weekend, most federal offices observe it on the nearest weekday (Saturday → Friday, Sunday → Monday) — shown as observed.

Stock-market closures (NYSE / Nasdaq)

U.S. equity markets follow their own calendar: they add Good Friday but do not close for Columbus Day or Veterans Day, and they hold shortened (1:00 pm ET) sessions around a few holidays. Rules can change — confirm the official schedule at nyse.com.

Daylight saving time

Most of the U.S. springs forward on the second Sunday of March and falls back on the first Sunday of November, both at 2:00 am local time. (Hawaii and most of Arizona do not observe DST.)

No network — everything happens on your machine. Federal-holiday and DST rules are U.S. law; market rules follow current NYSE/Nasdaq practice and are provided for convenience.
